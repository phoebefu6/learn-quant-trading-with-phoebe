Time-ordered splits

Sessions 3 and 4 asked what a number was the best of. Tonight asks a quieter question that ruins just as many backtests: were the days that scored the rule really unseen? A split decides which observations choose and which observations grade, and when observations span several days, a split that ignores time order lets the same days do both. Tonight reads the split code line by line, measures the leak on a world with nothing in it, removes it with a purge, and is honest about the one tool, the embargo, that this world cannot show. The data is synthetic throughout, and nothing here is investment advice.

Part 0

Marlow Street's confirmation that confirmed nothing

Marlow Street is a constructed research desk, four people and a head who has been handed a backtest with a Sharpe ratio of 1.8. After sessions 3 and 4 the researcher came back with what looked like the answer to every objection: the selection had been rerun under five-fold cross-validation, the same way the desk validates every classifier, and the kept rule still earned a positive Sharpe ratio on the held-out folds. The positions are held for five days. Tonight shows why that one detail turns a confirmation into a measurement of the split, and what it takes to make the held-out days genuinely held out.

LiveWhy the split is part of the experiment4 min▶

A held-out score is only as honest as the wall between the rows that chose and the rows that grade. On a table of customers that wall is easy: one row, one customer, and a shuffle puts each customer on one side. On a time series an observation is not a row in that sense. A five-day holding return made on Tuesday spans Wednesday to the following Tuesday, and the observation made on Wednesday spans four of the same five days. Put those two on opposite sides of the wall and the wall has a hole in it.

The rest is mechanics, and every mechanism tonight is measured on synthetic worlds where the alpha is zero by construction, so any out-of-sample figure above noise is the split talking, not the rule.

Real world

Marlow Street's researcher, constructed like the rest, used the desk's standard validation recipe: five folds, rows shuffled first, because that is how every churn and fraud model at the desk had been checked. Nobody asked whether a row was a day or a five-day span. When the head reran the recipe on 100 rules with nothing in them, across 100 generated worlds, the kept rule earned 0.65 out of sample on average. The confirmation had been confirming the split.

Self-studyThe words that keep coming back4 min▶
  • Observation. One position and what it earned. Observation t takes the position from the signal on day t and earns the return over the next h days.
  • Span. The days an observation earns over: days t + 1 to t + h. With h = 1 a span is one day and no two spans share a day.
  • Overlap. Observations i and j share h - |i - j| days whenever |i - j| is below h. With h = 5, neighbours share 4, 3, 2 and 1 days.
  • Walk-forward. Train on the first two thirds in time order, test on the rest. One fold, one cut.
  • Blocked k-fold. Cut the observations into k contiguous blocks; each block takes a turn as the test set while the others train.
  • Shuffled k-fold. Permute the observations at random, then cut the permutation into k folds. The default of most tabular tools.
  • Purge. Drop any training observation whose span shares a day with a test observation's span.
  • Embargo. Also drop a few training observations right after each test block, to absorb serial correlation that outlasts the span.

Part 1 · covers walk-forward against shuffled k-fold

What a shuffled k-fold leaks, and when

12 min live

Hold everything fixed that sessions 3 and 4 varied: zero alpha, 100 random rules, keep the best in-sample rule in each fold, score it on that fold's test observations, 100 worlds per row. Change only how the observations are divided and how long each one spans. The honest out-of-sample answer is known in advance to be noise around zero, so the only thing that can move it is the split.

LiveReading the first five rows4 min▶

Every row is zero alpha, 100 rules, 100 worlds. Read the out-of-sample column first, because it is the one with a known right answer: zero, give or take trial noise of about 0.07 on a mean over 100 worlds.

  • Walk-forward, one-day. 503 training and 252 test observations. The kept rule reads 1.78 in-sample and -0.05 out of sample. The baseline.
  • Shuffled five-fold, one-day, no purge. 604 and 151. In-sample 1.65, out of sample 0.10. Within trial noise of the baseline: the shuffle has nothing to leak.
  • Walk-forward, five-day. 500 and 251. In-sample 1.34, out of sample -0.07.
  • Blocked five-fold, five-day, no purge. 601 and 150. In-sample 1.23, out of sample -0.05.
  • Shuffled five-fold, five-day, no purge. 601 and 150. In-sample 1.11, out of sample 0.65. The leak.

The in-sample figures differ from row to row because each split trains on a different number and kind of observation, and session 3 showed that the best of 100 depends on both. The out-of-sample column is where the rows should agree, and four of the five do.

LiveWhy the shuffle leaks only when observations overlap4 min▶

Observation i earns over days i + 1 to i + h. Observation j earns over days j + 1 to j + h. Whenever |i - j| is less than h they share h - |i - j| days. With h = 5, a test observation shares 4 days with each immediate neighbour, 3 with the next, then 2, then 1.

A shuffle scatters the test observations through the whole history, so almost every test observation has training neighbours on both sides; in the first fold drawn above, every one of the 150 does. The selection loop then keeps the rule whose positions happened to line up with the returns on the training observations. Those returns are, day for day, most of the returns the test observations earn. The kept rule's luck on the training days is carried straight into the test score, and the score reports it as skill.

With h = 1 a span is a single day and no two observations share one. The shuffle still scatters the test set, but there is nothing on the other side of the wall to see. That is why the one-day shuffle reads 0.10, within trial noise of the walk-forward's -0.05, and the five-day shuffle reads 0.65. The leak is not shuffling as such. It is shuffling plus overlap.

Real world

The head at Marlow Street, constructed, asked one question that would have saved a week: how many days does one row of your validation set span? The researcher's answer was five. A desk whose habit is to ask that question before reading any cross-validated number catches this leak in a sentence; a desk without the habit can reach it only by rerunning the whole study on a world with nothing in it.

LiveThe split code, read exactly4 min▶

QT.splits(T, opts) returns a list of folds, each a list of training indices and a list of test indices over T observations. With 756 days and h-day holding there are 756 - h observations: 755 at h = 1, 751 at h = 5.

  • Walk-forward. The cut is the floor of T times two thirds. Everything before it trains, everything from it on tests, one fold. At T = 755 that is 503 and 252; at T = 751, 500 and 251.
  • Blocked. Fold f tests the observations from the floor of T f / k up to the floor of T (f + 1) / k, k = 5. At T = 751 the test blocks are 150, 150, 150, 150 and 151 long.
  • Shuffled. A Fisher-Yates permutation of 0 to T - 1, driven by the same integer mixer as everything else at the world's seed plus 2000. Fold f tests the f-th fifth of the permutation, so its test observations land anywhere in time.
  • Purge. When switched on, a training observation i is dropped if any test observation j sits within h - 1 of it, which is exactly the condition for their spans to share a day.
  • Embargo. When also set to e, training observation i is dropped if a test observation sits from h to h + e - 1 places before it. In words: the e observations just past the purge zone, after each test block, are dropped too.

The trial that uses the folds keeps the best of the 100 rules by per-observation Sharpe ratio on each fold's training indices, scores that rule on the fold's test indices, averages the per-observation ratios across folds, and annualises by the square root of 252 over h. The ladder averages 100 trials. The training and test sizes it prints are those of the first fold of the last world, which is why they are whole numbers and why the purged shuffle's figure is one fold's count, not an average.

One detail the walk-forward rows hide: with h = 5 and no purge, training observations 496 to 499 share days with test observations 500 to 503. The cut has a seam. Four observations out of 500 cannot carry a selection, and the -0.07 shows it, but the seam is real, and the homework purges it.

Self-studyWhy it is the default setting that leaks3 min▶

Shuffled k-fold is the default in most tabular tooling because it is right for the data those tools were built for: rows that are exchangeable, one customer or one transaction each, with nothing shared between them. A data team moving to time series brings that default with it, and on one-day observations it even appears to work, as the second row shows. It fails only when labels span more than one step, which is exactly when positions are held, returns are forward-looking, or a target is defined over a window.

Target leakage in general is owned elsewhere: learn-feature-engineering-with-phoebe, builder session 5 on leakage in tabular features, and learn-model-risk-with-phoebe, practitioner session 3 for the leak hunt on a single model. What is specific here is the mechanism: a split that is innocent on exchangeable rows becomes a leak the moment rows share days.

And the usual limit: the generator has no memory, so the kept rule earns nothing out of sample on the honest rows, not less than nothing. The 2014 Notices paper shows that with memory an overfit rule can do worse than zero; this bench does not model that case.

Part 2 · covers purging and the embargo

Purge the overlap, then ask what the embargo adds

12 min live

The fix follows from the mechanism. If a training observation's span shares a day with a test observation's span, drop the training observation. That is the purge. The embargo goes one step further and drops a few more training observations right after each test block, for dependence that outlasts the span. Three more rows of the same ladder show what each costs and what each buys on this world, and one of the answers is "nothing measurable".

LivePurging, measured on both kinds of split4 min▶

Shuffled five-fold, five-day, purged. Out of sample -0.02: the leak is gone. But the fold the ladder reports kept 141 training observations of 601. When test observations are scattered through the whole history, almost every training observation is within four places of one, so the purge throws away about three quarters of the training data. The count varies fold by fold and world by world, because it depends on where the shuffle happened to put the test observations; the build-along prints all five folds for one world.

The in-sample figure jumps to 2.77. Nothing improved. This is session 3's problem on a shorter ruler: the best of 100 estimates made on a small sample is large, because each estimate is noisier. A researcher who reports "after purging, in-sample went up" has reported the purge's cost as if it were a finding.

Blocked five-fold, five-day, purged. Out of sample 0.06, training observations 597 of 601. The test observations sit in one contiguous block, so only the four observations at each edge of the block share days with it. The purge costs 4 in the first fold, which has one edge, and 8 in the middle folds.

Which is why the cheap correct setting is blocked folds with a purge. A shuffled split with a purge is correct too, and expensive enough to make the in-sample number meaningless.

Real world

Marlow Street's researcher, constructed, purged the shuffled split, saw the in-sample figure climb from 1.11 to 2.77, and drafted a note saying the strategy was stronger than first thought. The head sent it back with one line: count the training rows. The desk's validation recipe for anything with a holding period is now blocked folds with a purge, and the note that explains why is two sentences long.

LiveThe embargo, as described and as measured4 min▶

As described. Lopez de Prado's Advances in Financial Machine Learning (2018), chapter 7, is reported to treat two corrections for cross-validation on financial series: purging, which drops training observations whose label span overlaps a test observation's span, and the embargo, which drops training observations immediately after a test block to absorb serial correlation that a purge at the span level does not reach. The book's chapter structure is reported from catalogue listings and its text was not read; the code here implements both from that description and from first principles, and nothing in it reproduces the book.

As measured. Blocked five-fold, five-day, purged, embargo of 5: 592 training observations, in-sample 1.24, out of sample -0.10. Against the purged row's 0.06 that looks like a move, but each mean carries trial noise of about 0.07 over 100 worlds, and both sit within noise of zero. The embargo changes nothing measurable here.

Why. The returns in this generator have no memory: each day is independent of every other once the span is accounted for. The purge already removes every training observation whose span shares a day with a test span, and there is no further dependence after the span for the embargo to absorb. So the honest sentence is that this bench cannot show the embargo working. That is a limit of the world, not a verdict on the tool. On a series whose dependence outlasts a label's span, the embargo is the guard for exactly that dependence, and its size has to be argued from the series, which is where the disagreement below begins.

Self-studyWhere serious people disagree4 min▶

How big an embargo. One position sizes the embargo from how long dependence lasts in the series, and accepts the lost training data as the price of a clean wall. The other holds that once label spans are purged correctly the embargo is insurance whose right size cannot be read off a backtest, and that an embargo tuned by looking at results is one more parameter chosen on the same history, which is the selection problem again. This bench cannot settle it, because it has no dependence for an embargo to catch.

Combinatorial purged cross-validation against a single walk-forward. The same book's chapter 12, by its reported structure, treats walk-forward testing, cross-validation and combinatorial purged cross-validation. The case for the combinatorial method: many purged train and test combinations give a distribution of out-of-sample paths rather than one, so a strategy is judged on more than one draw of history. The case for walk-forward: it is the only scheme that matches what a desk could have known at the time, and the many paths of the combinatorial method are cut from the same single history, so they are not independent evidence. Both camps agree on the purge. Which scheme to report is an open question, and it is taught here as one.

Part 3 · build-along

Find the shared days, purge them, measure the leak

18 min hands on

Open this page's console. The engine is loaded as QT. The steps rebuild Marlow Street's leak from the inside: one world's observations, one shuffled fold, the shared days counted by hand, the purge switched on, and then the leak row and its fix measured over 100 worlds each. Every step uses a canon seed, so your numbers will match for the same seed. Rolling-origin evaluation of a forecast, and the error metrics that go with it, live in learn-timeseries-forecasting-with-phoebe, builder session 8; here the thing evaluated is a selected strategy, not a forecast.

Count the observations on a five-day world

Seed 84,001 is the first world of the leak row. One random rule held for five days gives one observation per day that still has five days ahead of it: 756 days minus 5. Your numbers will match for the same seed.

consolevar w = QT.makeWorld({ trueSR: 0, seed: 84001 }); var x = QT.makeStrategies(w, { N: 1, seed: w.seed + 1000 })[0]; QT.pnl(w, x, { h: 5 }).length

Split those 751 observations the way the desk did

The shuffle seed is the world seed plus 2000, so 86,001 is the exact shuffle the leak row's first world uses. It prints five folds, 601 training and 150 test observations in the first, and the first eight test indices: 1, 2, 12, 13, 17, 19, 43, 51. Scattered from the very start of history.

consolevar f = QT.splits(751, { method: "shuffled", h: 5, purge: false, k: 5, seed: 86001 }); [f.length, f[0].train.length, f[0].test.length, f[0].test.slice(0, 8)]

Count the shared days by hand

Take test observation 1 and list its training neighbours within four places, each with the days it shares: 5 minus the distance. Observation 2 is also a test observation, so it is missing from the list. Then ask how many of the 150 test observations have at least one training neighbour. At this seed it prints all 150.

consolevar train = new Set(f[0].train), j = f[0].test[0], nb = []; for (var i = j - 4; i <= j + 4; i++) if (train.has(i)) nb.push([i, 5 - Math.abs(i - j)]); var touched = f[0].test.filter(function (t) { for (var d = 1; d < 5; d++) if (train.has(t - d) || train.has(t + d)) return true; return false; }).length; [JSON.stringify(nb), touched]

Switch the purge on and compare the training lengths

Same seed, so the same test observations; only the training side changes. Before the purge every fold trains on 600 or 601 observations. After it, the five folds keep 86, 64, 100, 88 and 84. That is the price of a purge on a shuffled split, fold by fold.

consolevar fp = QT.splits(751, { method: "shuffled", h: 5, purge: true, k: 5, seed: 86001 }); var len = function (folds) { return folds.map(function (g) { return g.train.length; }); }; [len(f), len(fp)]

Do the same on blocked folds, with and without an embargo

Blocked folds need no seed. Purged, the five folds keep 597, 593, 593, 593 and 596: four lost at each edge a test block touches. With an embargo of 5 they keep 592, 588, 588, 588 and 596: five more after each block that has training data after it, and none for the last block.

console[0, 5].map(function (e) { return len(QT.splits(751, { method: "blocked", h: 5, purge: true, embargo: e, k: 5 })); })

Now measure the leak and its fix over 100 worlds each

The split ladder's first seeds are 80,000 plus 1,000 times the row's position, counting from zero: 80,000 for walk-forward on one-day observations through 87,000 for the embargo row. The leak row is 84,000 and prints 1.11 in-sample and 0.65 out of sample; the purged row, 85,000, prints 2.77 and -0.02 with 141 training observations in the fold it reports. Together they take about a second.

consolevar leak = QT.ladderRung({ N: 100, trueSR: 0, method: "shuffled", h: 5, purge: false, k: 5 }, 100, 84000); var fixed = QT.ladderRung({ N: 100, trueSR: 0, method: "shuffled", h: 5, purge: true, k: 5 }, 100, 85000); [leak.isSR.toFixed(2), leak.oosSR.toFixed(2), fixed.isSR.toFixed(2), fixed.oosSR.toFixed(2), fixed.trainSize]

The habit to keep. Before reading any cross-validated or walk-forward number on a time series, ask two things: how many steps does one observation's label span, and how were the folds drawn? If the span is longer than one step and the folds were shuffled without a purge, the held-out score has measured the choosing, and nothing else about it needs reading.

Check yourself

Three questions

On zero alpha, 100 rules and 100 worlds, a shuffled five-fold split reads 0.10 out of sample on one-day observations and 0.65 on five-day observations. What makes the difference?

The leak needs overlap. Observations i and j share 5 - |i - j| days when they are fewer than 5 apart, and a shuffle puts nearly every test observation next to training ones. With one-day spans there is nothing to share, and 0.10 is within trial noise of the walk-forward's -0.05. Seeds move a mean by about 0.07 at 100 worlds, not by 0.55.

Purging the shuffled split brings the out-of-sample figure to -0.02, but the best in-sample figure rises from 1.11 to 2.77. What happened?

Nothing about the rules changed and nothing is real. Fewer training observations make each estimate noisier, and the maximum of 100 noisy estimates is higher. A purged blocked split loses 4 observations instead and its in-sample figure stays at 1.26.

Adding an embargo of 5 to the purged blocked split moves the out-of-sample figure from 0.06 to -0.10 and costs 5 more training observations. What is the honest reading?

A tool that cannot be shown on a world is not thereby shown to be useless. The embargo guards against dependence that outlasts a label's span, and this generator has none. Teach it as Lopez de Prado describes it (reported), and size it on a series that has the dependence it is meant to catch.

Homework

About forty minutes, in the console or in Python.

Purge the walk-forward seam

Call QT.splits on 751 observations with the walk-forward method, h of 5 and purge: true, and check that the training set lost exactly the four observations before the cut. Draw the leak as a function of the span
