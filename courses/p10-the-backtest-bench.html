The backtest bench

Nine sessions took the backtest apart one fault at a time. This one puts the whole generator in front of you with every dial exposed. Choose how many rules to try, whether one of them is real and how real, what a trade costs, and how the split is made; then read the in-sample figure beside the out-of-sample one, the deflated verdict beside the naive one, and the share of worlds where the real rule was the one you kept. There is a button that deploys the best in-sample rule, which is the mistake, and a button that shuffles time order inside the split, which breaks the bench on purpose and proves what it measures. Synthetic data only; nothing here is investment advice.

LiveWhat the two benches recompute on every press4 min▶

The selection bench builds a world, builds N rules, scores each on the first two thirds of the days, keeps the best, scores that one on the last third, and asks two questions of it: is its in-sample figure significant against zero, and is it significant against the expected maximum of N null trials. It does this for as many worlds as you ask and reports means with the count. If you plant a real rule, it also reports how often the rule you kept was the real one.

The split bench holds N at 100 and alpha at zero and changes only how the days are divided into training and test: by time, by contiguous blocks, or by shuffling, with and without a purge and an embargo. Nothing here describes a real market. The worlds are generated, the alpha is a dial, and the only real things in the page are the algorithms run over them.

LiveThe five runs worth doing first5 min▶
  • Leave everything alone and press deploy. 100 rules, nothing real, no cost. At 200 worlds the kept rule reports 1.80 in-sample and -0.06 out of sample, the naive test passes in 99.5 percent of worlds and the deflated test in none. The deploy button prints the two Sharpe figures side by side with the drop between them, which is what funding the best in-sample rule costs.
  • Take N to 1,000. In-sample 2.33, out of sample 0.02. The first number is a function of how hard you looked. The second is a function of what was there.
  • Plant a real 1.5 and keep N at 100. The real rule is the one you keep in 32.5 percent of worlds; the kept rule earns 0.46 out of sample; the deflated test passes in 0 percent of worlds. The haircut that killed every false positive also killed the real one.
  • Plant a real 3.0. Kept 90.5 percent of the time, out of sample 2.71, deflated passes 34.5 percent. And every world where the deflated test passed, the rule that passed was the real one: the false discovery rate after deflation is 0.000 on every planted rung.
  • Set N to 1, alpha to 1.0, and drag the cost to 20. A real edge of 1.0 with no competition reads 1.01 gross, 0.69 at 5 basis points, 0.37 at 10, and -0.30 at 20. Costs do to a real edge what selection does to a false one, from the other side.
Self-studyWhy the false discovery rate after deflation is exactly zero here4 min▶

Across every planted rung, every rule that cleared the deflated test was the planted one. That is not a coincidence of seeds. The deflated threshold is the expected maximum of N null trials, and a random rule has to beat not only that threshold but every other random rule to be the one kept. The two filters compound, and on this generator the product is zero false discoveries in 1,000 planted worlds.

The price is power. A real 1.5 among 100 competitors on two years of training data passes the deflated test in none of 200 worlds (1.5 to 3 percent on two other seed ranges); it needs to be the best of the hundred and clear 1.84, and it is only the best a third of the time. The ladder with more history shows the only cure: on 12 years the same real rule passes 45 percent of the time, on 24 years 94 percent. History cannot be manufactured, which is the whole content of the minimum backtest length in the 2014 Notices paper.

Be careful with the sentence "deflation works". It works at the thing it promises, which is false positives. A desk that reads "0 passes" as "there is nothing here" has confused a power problem with an absence. The honest sentence is: at this N and this sample length the test cannot distinguish a real 1.5 from noise, and no test can.

Self-studyThe canon seeds, so your 200 worlds are the canon 200 worlds3 min▶

Part 2 · the split bench

What shuffling time order leaks, and what purging recovers

16 min hands on

Hold N at 100 and alpha at zero, so that the honest out-of-sample answer is known to be nothing. Then change only how the days are divided. One of the settings is the default of every tabular machine-learning tool, and it is the one that reports a finding.

LiveThe four runs worth doing first5 min▶
  • Leave everything alone. Walk-forward, one-day holds, 100 worlds. The kept rule reports 1.78 in-sample and -0.05 out of sample: a search found the best of a hundred coin flips and the coin kept flipping.
  • Press the break button. It sets shuffled folds, five-day holds, no purge. Out of sample climbs to 0.65 on nothing. The split did not create alpha; it let the rule be scored on days it was chosen on. This is the setting every tabular tool produces by default, and it is the one that reports a finding.
  • Switch the purge on, leaving the shuffle. Out of sample falls back to -0.02 and the training set falls from 601 to 141 observations. The in-sample figure jumps to 2.77, because the best of a hundred estimates on 141 observations is a large number. Purging a shuffled split is correct and expensive.
  • Choose blocked folds, purge on, hold at 5. Out of sample 0.06, training 597. The purge costs four observations instead of 460. Then add an embargo of 5 and watch it change nothing measurable: the signal's serial correlation is already handled by the span-level purge on this world, and the honest statement is that the bench cannot show the embargo's effect here.
LiveReading the leak as a mechanism, not a number4 min▶

An observation with a five-day hold earns the sum of the next five days' returns. Two observations one day apart share four of those five days, and with an AR(1) signal they usually hold the same position, so their pnl is nearly the same number twice. Shuffle the days into folds and about a fifth of every test observation's neighbours are in the training set. The rule that was picked for doing well on those neighbours does well on the test observation for the same reason, and the out-of-sample figure stops measuring the future.

Set the hold to 1 and shuffle and nothing visible happens (0.10 against -0.05, within trial noise), because one-day observations share no days. The leak is not a property of shuffling. It is a property of shuffling observations that overlap, which is why the fix is the purge: remove from training anything whose span touches a test span, which is what Lopez de Prado's purged k-fold does, described in his 2018 book and implemented here from that description.

Self-studyWhat the bench cannot show, and says so3 min▶

The embargo exists to absorb serial correlation that outlives the span: features whose influence carries past the label window. On this generator the signal's memory is inside the span the purge already removes, so the embargo rows read -0.10 against 0.06, which is trial noise at 100 worlds. The right sentence is that the bench cannot show the embargo's effect on this world, not that the embargo does nothing. A world with slower features would show it, and building one is the homework.

The same honesty applies to the blocked split without a purge: -0.05, indistinguishable from the walk-forward's -0.07. The leak at block edges is real and too small to see at this size. A bench that reported it as zero would be reporting a limit of its own resolution as a fact about the method.

Part 3 · the brief

What you write down

11 min live

Both benches point at the same discipline: the number you present has to arrive with the things that bound it. Ten minutes, on a backtest you actually have, in four lines somebody else could check.

State the count and the expected best of that many

Not "a strategy with a Sharpe ratio of 1.8" but "the best of about 100 configurations on two years, where the expected best of 100 on nothing is 1.80". One sentence. If the count is unknown, write unknown, and notice that the number cannot then be read at all.

State the split and what it respects

"Walk-forward, train 503 days then test 252, five-day holds, purged at the boundary, no embargo." The second half of that sentence is the one that gets omitted, and it is the one that fails: a shuffled split on overlapping observations reports 0.65 on nothing.

State the costs and the net figure

"Gross 1.01, at 5 basis points per unit of turnover and 0.41 units a day, net 0.69." A real edge of 1.0 is gone by 15 basis points. The gross figure on its own is a number about a world without trading in it.

State the size and the median path

"At leverage 6 the mean terminal wealth over three years is 14.6 and the median is 4.8, with 98.5 percent of paths having lost half at some point." The mean alone, at leverage 12, is 71.5 with a median of 1.45. Which of those two numbers goes in the deck is the entire ethics of the job.

LiveThe two benches, on one card3 min▶

Selection. The best of N on nothing climbs with N and never stops: 1.04, 1.80, 2.33 for 10, 100, 1,000. The deflated test kills every one of them and, on two years of data, kills a real 1.5 too. The only input that restores power is history, which cannot be manufactured.

Splits. Shuffling time order lets a rule be scored on days it was chosen on, but only when observations overlap. The purge removes the leak on any split, and on a shuffled one it also removes most of the data. The cheap correct setting is blocked folds with a purge.

Both have the same shape: a quantity you can measure precisely, governed by a parameter you did not report, with a silent failure in between. That is the honest summary of working with a backtest, and it is more useful than either enthusiasm or dismissal.

After the course

Homework

About an hour, on something real.

Find N

For one backtest your organisation has funded, find the number of configurations tried before it. If nobody can produce one, that is the finding, and it means the headline was read without its bound.

Build the world that shows the embargo

Give the generator a signal whose influence outlives the hold: make the returns lean on the signal from two days back as well as one. Re-run the split bench and write down whether blocked-and-purged now leaks without an embargo. The bench on this page cannot show it; yours should.

Turn one defence off

Pick a control that is producing a clean result in a pipeline you own and disable the other one. If the result does not move, you have found out something true about your own system for the price of one run.

Check yourself

Three questions

You press "deploy the best in-sample rule" at N = 100 with nothing real. In-sample reads 1.80, out of sample reads -0.06. What does the drop measure?

The world is a random walk with no regime change, and the scorer is the same function on both halves. The in-sample figure equals the deflation threshold SR* by construction, because both are the expected best of N on nothing. The drop is the price of having chosen.

The break button sets shuffled folds, five-day holds and no purge, and the out-of-sample figure on nothing becomes 0.65. Set the hold to one day and the figure returns to noise. Why?

The shuffle does not create alpha. It lets the same returns appear on both sides of the split, which only happens when observations span more than one day. The purge removes exactly those neighbours, which is why it brings the figure back to -0.02.

A real 1.5 is planted among 100 rules on two years of training data. The deflated test passes in 0 of 200 worlds. What is the right reading?

The rule is there by construction. Loosening the test would re-admit the false positives it was built to stop, and the bench shows it admitted none. Power comes from independent observations, which is the minimum backtest length argument, and nothing else supplies it.
